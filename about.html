About

From the pool deck and the front door to the help desk.

Every job I’ve held has been about keeping systems running while people are counting on you. The systems are changing; the job is the same.

01

The short version

I spent the last several years in hospitality, aquatics, and physical security along the Texas Gulf Coast and in Atlanta. As a lead supervisor at Schlitterbahn I ran communication systems, scheduling software, and safety data logs for a team of lifeguards. As a Securitas officer I controlled access to restricted areas and verified credentials. As a server and bartender I kept POS systems running through high-volume shifts and fixed transaction errors on the spot.

Along the way I became the person coworkers came to when a terminal froze, the Wi‑Fi dropped, or a report would not export. I liked that part of the job more than the rest of it, so I am making it the job.

Right now

  • Studying for the CompTIA A+ (220-1201 / 220-1202)
  • Building a home lab with a Windows domain, Linux hosts, and virtualization, mentored by a senior systems engineer in the family
  • Looking for a help desk, desktop support, or NOC role where I can learn a real environment
02

Why security

Background

I already think in access control

Who is allowed in, how do we verify it, what do we log, what happens when something is off. Physical security taught me the questions; network security just changes the doors.

Temperament

Steady when it’s loud

Lifeguarding and bartending are both about staying calm and methodical while everything around you is urgent. Incident response is the same skill.

Direction

The technical lane

My path runs help desk to sysadmin to security operations: Network+, Security+, then CySA+ and hands-on blue-team work. The plan is on the Security Path page.

03

How I work

  • Write it down. If I fixed it once, the next person should be able to fix it from my notes.
  • Own the ticket. The user does not care whose queue it is in; I follow it until it is closed.
  • Ask early. Escalating at minute five beats guessing for an hour.
  • Learn in public. Lab builds and study notes go on this site and on GitHub, mistakes included.
